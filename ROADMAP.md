# Roadmap

Next, in order.

1. **Carrier survival.**  The bots steal at the players' rate and convert
   one steal in five where Zest converts nearly one in two.  Escorts that
   screen the carrier's line rather than trail it, routes home that avoid
   the defenders' known lines, and armor before the run.
2. **More of the players per map.**  Beyond bites: route samples (where the
   good players actually go, as a small discount on those cells) and fight
   samples (position and weapon), from the same demos and from play.
3. **The rope from the air.**  The players fire 60% of their ropes airborne;
   the bots mostly from the floor.  Rides chosen and fired mid-flight, and a
   rope rate held at the players' rhythm on every map (the rate ran to 54 a
   minute on smap26).
4. **The last lava deaths.**  Rides whose live arc passes over harm on
   bctf01-type maps: a stricter ride record where the arc crosses hazard,
   and rescue anchors chosen for the drop they leave.
5. **Turtle and posts, watched live.**  The turtle goal confirmed in a live
   game now that captures are tallied; defender posts that sit on ledges
   with no floor for footwork moved to the nearest floor with the same
   cover.
6. **A regression gate.**  After every game, the same table against Zest,
   Lequin and the owner per map, kept with the logs, so a change that costs
   rope rate or captures shows the same day.
7. **Aim and skill.**  The bots rail humans hard (the owner died to rails
   twenty-one times in one match); sg_skill and the personas tuned against
   the players' accuracy from the demos.
8. **Every map validated.**  All 181 maps of the client's paks have runes;
   a pass per map for unreachable samples, stuck spots and mechanisms
   (doors, buttons, lifts) the bots cannot work.
9. **Windows on a real server.**  The module cross-builds and stands alone;
   it has not run on Windows yet.  A 32-bit build if anyone still needs it.
10. **Presentation.**  Chatter by persona, the callouts trimmed to what a
    teammate wants to hear, ping and stats as ordinary clients.
